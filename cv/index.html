Research Experience

  • 2024--present
    Graduate Student
    UC San Diego Visual Learning Lab (Principal Investigator: Bria Long)
    How the visual input children actually receive shapes the object representations they build, and how that input compares to what vision models are trained on.
    • What objects children see, and how that differs from curated datasets. Object frequency and representational geometry across 868+ hours of egocentric video (BabyView, 31 families) compared against THINGS. Methods: YOLOE detection with CLIP-based filtering, CLIP and DINOv3 embeddings, representational similarity analysis. Output: first-author manuscript under review; public reproducibility capsule.
    • Why some categories are visually harder than others. Global and local dispersion measures over 85 categories separate viewpoint-driven from instance-driven variability; rankings partially replicate across backbones and are only weakly coupled to frequency. Output: CCN 2026 spotlight; reproducible analysis package and layer-wise embedding explorer.
    • Whether children and models converge on 3D shape. MOCHI Kids, a developmental (ages 4–6) adaptation of the MOCHI shape-oddity benchmark, built and deployed for direct human-model comparison on multiview object consistency. Methods: TypeScript and jsPsych client, Express and MongoDB backend, end-to-end tests in CI. Status: piloting.
    • How language and vision align in everyday input. Alignment between child-directed speech and co-occurring visual scenes in naturalistic video, using multimodal models as the measure. Output: co-first-author preprint.
    • What changes when children act on objects. Identifying viewed and manipulated objects by combining monocular depth estimation, IMU-derived head direction, and hand tracking; building a synchronized capture rig (Pupil Labs Neon eye trackers, two depth cameras, room-view camera) for caregiver-child interaction studies. Status: in progress.
  • 2022--2024
    Lab Technician
    UT Austin Developing Intelligence Lab (Principal Investigator: Chen Yu)
    • Multimodal measurement of infant-caregiver interaction. Ran home-based sessions with 12–36 month infants using head-mounted eye trackers, third-person cameras, IMUs, and lapel microphones. Methods: Python preprocessing pipelines across devices; MATLAB codebase to analyze and visualize multi-stream data; YOLOv8 fine-tuning for infant and parent views; MediaPipe and OpenPose for hand and face detection; WhisperX for transcription and diarization; skeletal tracking for dyadic play; 3D environments in Unity, Blender, and Matterport.
